# Peculiar Pioneers — Ten Bible Studies

Prepared September 14, 2026.

The study center now has ten selectable subjects. Each has seven coordinated learning points, seven flashcards, and seven four-option questions. The readings teach the material tested in the cards and quizzes. Personal reflection prompts are not scored.

**Source scope:** Selected passages from *The Great Controversy*, chapters 18–42, and *Steps to Christ*, with additional material from the introduction to *The Great Controversy*, *The Ministry of Healing*, *The Desire of Ages*, and *Education*. This is an introductory topic collection, not an exhaustive course through every chapter of those books.

**Quotation policy:** Each subject contains seven readings, seven cards, and seven quiz questions. Scripture memory texts are KJV; shortened quotations are labeled as excerpts. Lesson readings, explanations, and flashcard answers are editorial summaries, not verbatim EGW quotations. The biblical references and EGW book/page references accompany each learning point. The lesson on October 22 distinguishes the biblical feast date from the 1844 calendar correspondence stated in GC 399.4.

## Learning experience

- **Read & reflect:** Seven expandable explanations with KJV links, EGW references, and personal reflection prompts.
- **Flashcards:** Seven questions and answers, previous/next controls, a visible card count, and references on the answer side.
- **Check understanding:** Seven questions with four choices each. A submitted answer is locked, the correct answer is emphasized, and a source-backed explanation appears.
- **Results:** First-answer scoring, targeted reading links for missed points, and a retake that resets only the selected quiz.
- **Continuity:** Answers remain available while navigating between topics during the current preview session; durable accounts and progress synchronization are future implementation work.

## Righteousness by Faith

Christ’s righteousness, assurance, living faith, and loving obedience.

> For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man should boast.

*Ephesians 2:8–9 · KJV*

### 1. The righteousness we cannot supply

**Read & reflect**

We cannot erase past sin or supply the righteousness God’s law requires by our own efforts. Christ lived a sinless life and died for us. He offers His righteousness to those who give themselves to Him and receive Him as their Saviour. Our hope begins with His provision.

**Reflect:** What changes when you stop treating acceptance with God as something to earn?

**Flashcard**

**Question:** Why do sinners need Christ’s righteousness?

**Answer:** We have no righteousness of our own that can meet the claims of God’s holy law.

**Check understanding**

What meets the sinner’s need for righteousness before God?

- **A.** The righteousness provided by Jesus Christ.
- **B.** Religious effort sufficient to make Christ unnecessary.
- **C.** A comparison showing fewer sins than someone else.
- **D.** An improved reputation that erases the past.

**Correct answer: A. The righteousness provided by Jesus Christ.**

**Explanation:** We cannot erase past sin or supply the righteousness God’s law requires by our own efforts. Christ lived a sinless life and died for us. He offers His righteousness to those who give themselves to Him and receive Him as their Saviour. Our hope begins with His provision.

**Why the other choices do not fit:**

- **B:** The source identifies our need for Christ’s righteousness.
- **C:** The standard is God’s holy law, not another person’s record.
- **D:** A reputation cannot remove guilt or supply perfect righteousness.

**Sources**

KJV: [Romans 3:20–24 · KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%203%3A20-24&version=KJV); [Isaiah 64:6 · KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Isaiah%2064%3A6&version=KJV)

When we surrender to Christ and accept Him, His character stands in place of ours, and we are accepted before God for His sake. This is no boast in personal merit. Justification gives peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ and directs all gratitude to Him.

**Reflect:** How does acceptance for Christ’s sake answer both pride and despair?

**Answer:** Christ’s righteousness; His character stands in place of ours.

**Check understanding**

**Correct answer: B. Christ’s character and righteousness received by faith.**

**Explanation:** When we surrender to Christ and accept Him, His character stands in place of ours, and we are accepted before God for His sake. This is no boast in personal merit. Justification gives peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ and directs all gratitude to Him.

**Why the other choices do not fit:**

- **A:** The invitation is to the sinner who gives himself to Christ and accepts Him.
- **C:** Acceptance rests in Christ, not another person’s approval.

### 6. Grace bears fruit in love

**Read & reflect**

Salvation is the free gift of God, received by faith. Obedience and service are its fruit. As love for Christ fills the heart, we desire to please Him and bless others. A living faith neither boasts in works nor uses grace to excuse disobedience.

**Flashcard**

**Answer:** They are the fruit of living faith and gratitude for grace, not the purchase price of salvation.

**Check understanding**

- **B.** Works matter chiefly because they give us grounds to boast.
- **C.** Grace removes any purpose for good works.
- **D.** We receive salvation by faith, and that living faith produces loving obedience.

**Correct answer: D. We receive salvation by faith, and that living faith produces loving obedience.**

**Explanation:** Salvation is the free gift of God, received by faith. Obedience and service are its fruit. As love for Christ fills the heart, we desire to please Him and bless others. A living faith neither boasts in works nor uses grace to excuse disobedience.

- **A:** Ephesians places salvation in God’s gift of grace through faith.

- **C:** Ephesians 2:10 describes a life created in Christ for good works.

Trust in Christ gives reason for gratitude and hope. Steps to Christ invites us to remember God’s mercies and represent His goodness to others. - **D.** Treat a sad feeling as proof that Christ has rejected you.

**Correct answer: A. Acknowledge the trial, bring it to God, and remember His faithful love.**

## Victory Over Sin

Repentance, surrender, abiding in Christ, and a changed life.

> For without me ye can do nothing.

*John 15:5 · excerpt · KJV*

### 1. Repentance reaches the heart

**Read & reflect**

True repentance includes sorrow for sin and turning away from it. It goes deeper than fear of punishment. Christ draws us to Himself and gives the repentance we cannot produce by our own strength. David sought a clean heart as well as pardon.

**Reflect:** Is there a wrong you have regretted mainly because of its consequences?

**Flashcard**

**Question:** What distinguishes true repentance from regret over consequences?

**Answer:** Sorrow for sin itself and a turning away from it, through Christ’s grace.

**Check understanding**

Which response most clearly expresses true repentance?

- **A.** Regret being discovered while planning to repeat the wrong.
- **B.** Blame circumstances so personal guilt can be avoided.
- **C.** Ask God for a clean heart and turn from the sin itself.
- **D.** Wait to become worthy before coming to Christ.

**Correct answer: C. Ask God for a clean heart and turn from the sin itself.**

**Explanation:** True repentance includes sorrow for sin and turning away from it. It goes deeper than fear of punishment. Christ draws us to Himself and gives the repentance we cannot produce by our own strength. David sought a clean heart as well as pardon.

**Why the other choices do not fit:**

- **A:** Regret over consequences is not a turning away from sin.
- **B:** Repentance honestly acknowledges the wrong.
- **D:** Christ Himself draws us and gives repentance.

**Sources**

KJV: [Acts 3:19 · KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts%203%3A19&version=KJV); [Psalm 51:1–10 · KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm%2051%3A1-10&version=KJV); [Romans 2:4 · KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%202%3A4&version=KJV)

**Reflect:** What decision can you consciously place under Christ’s direction today?

**Answer:** Yield our will to Christ and depend on His transforming power.

**Check understanding**

- **B.** Expect willpower alone to regenerate the heart.
- **C.** Wait until every desire changes before choosing God.
- **D.** Choose to yield to Christ and cooperate with His working within.

**Correct answer: D. Choose to yield to Christ and cooperate with His working within.**

**Explanation:** We cannot change the heart by unaided effort, but we can choose to yield our will to God. As we surrender to Christ, He works within us to will and to do His pleasure. Victory involves real choice and cooperation with His power, not passive permission for sin to continue.

**Why the other choices do not fit:**

- **C:** We may choose to yield while depending on Him to change the desires.
